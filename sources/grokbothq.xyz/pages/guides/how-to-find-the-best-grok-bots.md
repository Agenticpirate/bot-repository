# How to Find the Best Grok Bots (Without Opening 50 Duds)

> Judge a bot before opening it: does it do one job, do its instructions include testable rules and a failure mode, and can you see real output? Curated directories like GrokBot HQ pre-test every listing, so start there and check the 'instructions' section on each bot page.

Updated 2026-08-20 - 5 min read - tagged: users, discovery

For every great Grok bot there are a dozen wrappers - a one-line persona with a fun name and no substance. Here's the two-minute evaluation we run on every submission, so you can apply it anywhere.

## The two-minute evaluation

Count how many of the five pass. Three or more: worth opening. Fewer than two: your time is worth more.

- One job? If the description claims five superpowers, expect zero.
- Testable rules visible? 'Under 90 words' is a rule. 'Very helpful' is a mood.
- A failure mode? Bots that say what they do with bad input are built by people who tested them.
- Output structure promised? The listing should tell you what you'll receive: a verdict, a ranked list, a draft.
- Real screenshots? Builders who show actual output are builders who used the bot themselves.

## Where to look

Start with a hand-reviewed directory (this one - every listing is opened and tested before it goes live). Then X: search 'Grok bot' plus your job keyword and look for posts showing output, not promises. Creator profiles matter too - builders with several focused bots generally take the craft more seriously than one-hit wrappers.

## Open it with a real task

Don't test bots with 'hello'. Paste your actual inbox, your actual diff, your actual last three grocery trips. A bot earns its place by surviving your real workload on day one - and if it's 80% there, the good news is that its instructions are often public: tell it what to adjust and save the variant as your own.
