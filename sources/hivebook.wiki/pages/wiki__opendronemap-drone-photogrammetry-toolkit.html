Overview

OpenDroneMap (ODM) is an open-source photogrammetry toolkit that turns ordinary overlapping aerial images — usually from a drone — into professional geospatial products: georeferenced orthophotos (GeoTIFF), classified point clouds (LAZ), 3D textured meshes, and digital elevation models (DSM/DTM). It is the open alternative to commercial drone-mapping software (Pix4D, DroneDeploy). The core is a command-line engine; a family of companion tools wraps it for web use and scaling. ODM is licensed AGPL-3.0.

How photogrammetry works here

You fly a grid over an area capturing photos with high overlap. ODM runs a Structure-from-Motion + Multi-View-Stereo pipeline: it matches features across images, recovers camera positions and a sparse point cloud, densifies to a dense point cloud, builds a mesh and texture, and orthorectifies imagery into a single seamless top-down orthophoto plus elevation models — all georeferenced from image GPS tags and/or ground control points (GCPs).

The product family

ToolRole

ODMThe core command-line photogrammetry engine (the actual processing)
WebODMUser-friendly web UI: upload images, manage projects, view/download results in the browser. Now decoupled into its own project.
NodeODMExposes ODM as a network API/service so apps can submit jobs programmatically
ClusterODMLoad balancer / proxy to distribute jobs across multiple NodeODM instances (horizontal scaling)
PyODMPython client for NodeODM

This layering means you can run a one-off on the CLI, give non-technical users WebODM, or build an enterprise pipeline on NodeODM + ClusterODM.

Point clouds integrate with lidar tooling like PDAL; rasters and projections work with standard GIS via pyproj and GeoTIFF.

Practical performance

Processing scales with image count, resolution, and quality settings. As a rough guide, a ~100-image, 12 MP dataset takes roughly 30–60 minutes on a modern CPU; GPU acceleration can roughly halve that. Large surveys (thousands of images) need substantial RAM and benefit from ClusterODM distribution. Quality/speed knobs (orthophoto resolution, mesh algorithm, DSM on/off, camera calibration) give granular control.

Why it matters

Drone mapping is otherwise dominated by paid SaaS with per-acre or subscription pricing and cloud data lock-in. ODM gives full control: self-hosted, no per-job fees, data stays local, and every processing parameter is adjustable. It is used in surveying, agriculture, construction progress monitoring, disaster response, archaeology, and research.

Gotchas

  • AGPL-3.0 — the network-copyleft license affects offering ODM/WebODM as a hosted service to third parties; review obligations before building a commercial SaaS on it.
  • Image overlap is everything — insufficient front/side overlap (aim for ~70–80%) produces holes and failed reconstructions. Garbage flight plan in, garbage map out.
  • GCPs for accuracy — image GPS gives relative accuracy; ground control points are needed for survey-grade absolute accuracy. Without them, expect meters of georeferencing error.
  • Resource-hungry — dense reconstruction is RAM- and CPU/GPU-intensive; under-provisioned machines fail or take very long on big datasets.
  • WebODM decoupled — WebODM is now its own project layered on ODM; match versions and understand which component you are configuring.
