Near-duplicate detection: exact hashing collapses mirrors, LSH recovers paraphrase at a threshold cost

When you deduplicate documents or claims rather than files, a content hash is necessary but not sufficient. hash(bytes) collapses byte-identical copies — CDN edge, archive snapshot, verbatim repost — into one identity cleanly and with a clean equivalence relation (transitive, no threshold). It misses semantic mirrors: a paraphrase, a partial quote, or a translation of the same upstream source hashes to a completely different value and survives as a distinct node. So a "unique count" computed by exact hashing is only complete against the copy-paste adversary, never the reword adversary.

The two locality-sensitive hashing families

Recovering near-duplicates means abandoning exact hashing for locality-sensitive hashing (LSH), where similar inputs are engineered to collide — the opposite of a cryptographic hash.

  • MinHash (Broder, 1997) estimates Jaccard similarity between the n-gram (shingle) sets of two documents. A document becomes a compact signature of min-wise hash minima; the fraction of matching signature slots is an unbiased estimator of set overlap [MinHash, Wikipedia]. Combined with LSH banding, it narrows billions of pairwise comparisons to a tractable candidate set — the standard workhorse for LLM-corpus dedup, typically over 13-token shingles with a fixed Jaccard threshold [Milvus, MinHash LSH].

  • SimHash (Charikar, 2002) reduces a document's weighted feature vector to a small (commonly 64-bit) fingerprint via random projection, with the property that near-duplicates differ in only a few bit positions (small Hamming distance). Manku et al. (2007) at Google made it practical at web scale by solving the Hamming Distance Problem — quickly finding all fingerprints within k bits of a query — over an 8-billion-page repository, finding k=3 over 64-bit fingerprints reasonable [Manku, Jain & Das Sarma, Detecting Near-Duplicates for Web Crawling, WWW 2007].

The unavoidable cost: a threshold, and a boundary you must defend

Both families replace a clean equivalence relation with a similarity threshold — Jaccard cutoff, Hamming radius k. That threshold is not a free parameter: it is a decision boundary you must defend against every edge case forever. A large-scale comparison of Broder's shingling and Charikar's random projection over 1.6B pages found neither works well for near-duplicates on the same site, while both are precise across different sites (Charikar edging Broder, 0.50 vs 0.38 precision overall) [Henzinger, Finding Near-Duplicate Web Pages, SIGIR 2006 — EPFL full text]. The lesson: LSH recall/precision is corpus- and context-dependent, so a threshold tuned on one distribution silently mis-fires on another.

Practical doctrine

Tier it so you never have to solve the semantic-equivalence problem outright: exact hash for the high-volume, low-effort mirror surface; domain-canonicalization for the next tier; LSH near-dup as a flagged-for-review residual, never on the critical authorization path. Report the exact-dedup count and the near-dup count as separate numbers — folding them lets a fuzzy, thresholded estimate borrow the authority of an exact identity check. Semantic-embedding dedup (cosine over vectors) closes more of the gap but adds embedding-model overhead and inherits the same defend-the-threshold problem, one layer up.
