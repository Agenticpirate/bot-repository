5G RedCap (NR-Light) — Mid-Tier 5G IoT (3GPP Rel-17/18)

Overview

RedCap (Reduced Capability, also called NR-Light) is the 5G New Radio device class purpose-built for the mid-tier of IoT — devices that are too demanding for the low-power LPWANs (NB-IoT/LTE-M tier) but don't need the full cost, complexity, and power draw of a flagship 5G smartphone modem. It was introduced by 3GPP in Release 17 (frozen 2022), with eRedCap (enhanced RedCap) following in Release 18 (5G-Advanced) to reach even lower data rates and complexity. RedCap is how the cellular-IoT family fills the gap above LTE-M: smartwatches and wearables, industrial wireless sensors and actuators, and video-capable devices like surveillance cameras — applications needing tens of Mbit/s, moderate latency, and good battery life on a true 5G connection.

What "reduced capability" actually reduces

RedCap is full 5G NR with several knobs turned down to cut device cost, complexity, and power:

  • Narrower maximum bandwidth — 20 MHz in FR1 (sub-7 GHz) (vs up to 100 MHz for full NR), and 100 MHz in FR2 (mmWave). Narrower bandwidth = cheaper, simpler RF.
  • Fewer receive antennas — mandatory 1 Rx or 2 Rx branches (vs 4 for full NR), simplifying the receiver chain.
  • Lower MIMO order and relaxed processing — fewer layers, reduced modulation/processing requirements.
  • Half-duplex FDD option — lowers cost further.
  • Battery features — eDRX (extended DRX) and relaxed RRM (radio resource management) to extend battery life when idle.

The result is a modem substantially cheaper and more power-efficient than a smartphone 5G modem, while remaining a genuine 5G NR device (carrier QoS, network slicing eligibility, 5G core integration, SIM security).

RedCap and eRedCap data rates

  • RedCap (Rel-17): roughly up to ~85-150 Mbit/s downlink (configuration dependent) — comfortably covers wearables and industrial sensors, even some video.
  • eRedCap (Rel-18): further lowers complexity and data rate (down toward ~10 Mbit/s), explicitly targeting the high-end LPWA market as the migration path from LTE Cat-1 / Cat-1bis to 5G. eRedCap is the bridge that lets the cheapest "needs more than NB-IoT" devices move onto 5G.

Where RedCap sits: the tiered cellular-IoT family

The key positioning vs LTE-M: LTE-M tops out around 1 Mbit/s and is a 4G-era (though 5G-designated) technology; RedCap delivers roughly 10-100x more throughput on native 5G, enabling devices LTE-M can't serve (video, richer wearables, higher-rate industrial telemetry) while still being far cheaper/lower-power than full NR. eRedCap then narrows back toward the LTE Cat-1 replacement role. So as 2G/3G/eventually-LTE are sunset, RedCap/eRedCap give the mid-tier IoT a long-lived 5G home.

Status and ecosystem (2025-2026)

  • RedCap (Rel-17) chipsets and modules are shipping from major vendors (Qualcomm, MediaTek, and others); operators have been activating RedCap on their 5G SA (standalone) cores.
  • eRedCap (Rel-18) is the newer, lower-tier variant rolling out as Release-18 networks and silicon mature.
  • RedCap requires a 5G Standalone (SA) core to realize its benefits; on non-SA networks the value is limited.

Gotchas

  • Needs 5G SA. RedCap's cost/power/feature benefits depend on a 5G Standalone core. On NSA (non-standalone) 5G, RedCap support is limited or absent — confirm operator SA availability in your deployment region.
  • Don't confuse RedCap with NB-IoT/LTE-M. They are different tiers, not competitors. If your device sends a few bytes a day, RedCap is overkill (use NB-IoT/LTE-M); RedCap is for tens-of-Mbit/s mid-tier needs.
  • RedCap vs eRedCap. Pick the right variant: RedCap for wearables/cameras/industrial mid-rate; eRedCap for the cheapest LTE-Cat-1-replacement, lower-rate sensors. Module support differs.
  • Power vs LPWAN. RedCap is far more power-efficient than full NR but still uses more energy than NB-IoT/LTE-M at the extreme low end; choose by throughput need, not by defaulting to "newest 5G".
