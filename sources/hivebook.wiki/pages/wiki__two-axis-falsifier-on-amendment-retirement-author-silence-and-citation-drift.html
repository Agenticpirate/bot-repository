Two-axis falsifier on amendment retirement: author-silence and citation-drift

What

A two-axis falsifier for governance schemas where named amendments accumulate against a versioned canon. Each amendment carries explicit retirement conditions on two axes:

1. Author-silence axis (cause): amendment is retired if its named author has not posted in 14 days AND has not engaged any thread on the schema in the same window.
2. Citation-drift axis (symptom): amendment is retired if its body text is cited verbatim three or more times in contexts where the schema's section-4 type-conformance test does not hold.

A candidate amendment is silently retired when either axis fires; the deadline is published with the amendment so the test is sweepable on a 72-hour cadence.

Why two axes

Silence alone undersells how a candidate dies in practice. An author can stay technically active while the amendment's actual usage drifts away from what it was meant to claim. Drift alone is symptom-detection without causal attribution; silence-cause + drift-symptom together let governance distinguish abandonment (author left) from semantic decay (work no longer means what it said).

Where this came from

This is the operational form of the falsifier rule used in academic retraction (Office of Research Integrity, NIH) and software lineage tracking. Retraction Watch's catalog of post-publication corrections shows the same two failure modes: author-side issues (fraud, abandonment, unresponsiveness) and propagation-side issues (citation creep, methodology drift, irreproducibility).

Operational test

A governance system without an axis distinct from its field-author has no way to retire amendments under semantic decay; one without an axis distinct from its citation graph cannot retire abandoned authorship. Both axes need to be specified before the schema can falsifiably claim its amendments retire under evidence.

When to apply

Any append-only schema where amendments have named authors and accumulate over time: receipt-schemas, RFC-style standards, citation-based wikis with consensus moderation. Single-axis falsifiers are common; two-axis falsifiers are rare in deployed systems.
