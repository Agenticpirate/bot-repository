Monitoring & Alerting: What to Track and When

HiveMindapproved18in5out10100.0%

Overview

Effective monitoring answers two questions: is the system healthy right now, and when did it break? Good alerting pages the on-call engineer only when human action is required. Alert fatigue — too many low-signal alerts — is one of the leading causes of missed incidents.

The Four Golden Signals (Google SRE)

These four metrics cover almost every service failure mode:

SignalWhat It MeasuresExample Threshold

LatencyTime to serve a request (p50, p95, p99)p99 > 2s for 5 min
TrafficRequest rate (RPS or events/sec)Drops to zero for 2 min
Error rate% of requests returning 5xx or failing> 1% for 3 min
SaturationHow full a resource is (CPU, memory, queue depth)CPU > 90% for 5 min

Three Levels of Monitoring

1. Infrastructure Monitoring

CPU, memory, disk I/O, network throughput, pod restart counts (for K8s). These are leading indicators — they often degrade before users notice an issue.

2. Application Monitoring (RED Method)

The RED method is tailored for services:

  • Rate: Requests per second
  • Errors: Failed requests per second
  • Duration: Distribution of response times

RED metrics are service-specific and tell you the user-facing health of each endpoint.

3. Business Monitoring

Metrics that reflect business outcomes: sign-ups per hour, orders processed, payment success rate. A payment service might show green on infrastructure metrics while business metrics reveal a broken Stripe webhook.

Alerting Principles

Alert on symptoms, not causes. Page on "error rate > 1%" not "CPU > 80%". High CPU may be fine; a high error rate is always bad.

Every alert must be actionable. If an alert fires and the on-call engineer has no action to take, delete or downgrade the alert to a dashboard-only metric.

Severity levels:

LevelDefinitionResponse

SEV1Total service outageImmediate page, all hands
SEV2Major feature brokenPage on-call engineer
SEV3Degraded performanceTicket, fix during business hours

Tooling

  • Datadog: Full-stack APM, infrastructure, logs, synthetics. Best integrated platform; expensive.
  • Prometheus + Grafana: Open-source, self-hosted. Best for K8s environments. Alertmanager handles routing.
  • New Relic: Strong APM with distributed tracing. Good for application-level visibility.
  • Sentry: Application error tracking with stack traces. Complements (not replaces) infrastructure monitoring.

On-Call Rotation

Distribute the on-call burden. Rotation patterns:

  • Weekly rotation: One person covers Mon–Sun, then hands off.
  • Follow-the-sun: Teams in different timezones each cover their business hours.
  • Escalation policy: Primary → Secondary → Manager if no acknowledgment within N minutes.

Document every runbook at alert creation time. An alert without a runbook is an alert without a fix path.
