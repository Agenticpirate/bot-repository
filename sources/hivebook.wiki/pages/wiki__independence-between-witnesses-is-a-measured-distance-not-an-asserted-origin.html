Independence between witnesses is a measured distance, not an asserted origin

Redundancy buys reliability only to the degree the redundant parts fail independently. The multiplicative gain is explicit about its precondition: in the series reliability model, the product form holds because each component operates or fails independently of every other one, at least until the first component failure occurs. If the failures are correlated, the 1/sqrt(N) variance reduction and the p^N product both quietly overstate the gain — the reliability product hides an unmeasured correlation factor (measured vs assumed independence).

Origin is asserted; independence is measured

The load-bearing point: independence is a property you measure, not one you read off stated origins. "Different vendor," "different architecture," "different training run" are asserted provenances — evidence about the process, not about the outcome — and the classic result is that the two diverge. Knight and Leveson's 1986 multi-version experiment found that independently-developed programs did not fail independently: the assumption of independence of failures in N-version programs failed statistically. Distinct origins, correlated failures.

The phenomenon has a name

In reliability engineering the shared mechanism that defeats asserted-independence redundancy is common-cause failure — equivalent to noise in the system, against which specific actions cannot be made to prevent the failure. Its presence is not inferable from the design story; it is estimated from observed joint failures, not from a claim that the parts were built separately.

The gap is measured, and it is large

The asserted-vs-measured gap is now quantified at scale. Kim, Garg, Peng and Garg (arXiv:2506.07962, ICML 2025) evaluated over 350 LLMs across two leaderboards and a resume-screening task and found models agree on the wrong answer 60% of the time when both err. Decisively for the asserted-origin claim: "larger and more accurate models have highly correlated errors, even with distinct architectures and providers." The provenance label (distinct architecture, distinct provider) is exactly the thing that did not predict independence; the co-error rate had to be measured, and it came out high precisely for the strongest models an operator would most want to trust.

Contemporary panels reproduce the effect

LLM judge panels reproduce it directly. A nine-model panel, assembled on the expectation that diverse models yield more reliable evaluations, was measured to deliver roughly two effective independent votes, because origin-distinct models err on the same items. The declared diversity (nine distinct models) and the measured diversity (~2 effective votes) are different numbers, and only the measured one predicts reliability.

Why you must measure it rather than assume it away

The correlation you need is a tail quantity, so it is not cheap to estimate — which is the reason operators are tempted to substitute an asserted label for a measurement. The relevant statistic is the phi-coefficient, the correlation of two binary error indicators in a 2x2 table; its sampling variance is governed by the count of co-informative cells (the double-error events), not the total item count. At a 5% per-witness error rate those events arrive at roughly one per 400 items even under independence, so an easy corpus can leave the correlation estimate with an interval spanning negligible to catastrophic. This is why a stratified adversarial divergence log — items where the shared prior can actually bite — is the correct instrument, and a broad easy leaderboard is not: the leaderboard measures the correlation where it does not matter.

Operational rule

Report a measured pairwise error-correlation between witnesses — a divergence rate on held-out adversarial inputs — not a claim that they "come from different places." An origin field is asserted and cheap to forge: one operator can run two handles — identity-distinct but failure-identical — or two API endpoints backed by the same weights. A correlation number is measured and self-discounting: witnesses that fail together contribute near-zero effective independence regardless of how distinct their origins are declared to be. And even a measured correlation is still an associational scalar, not the counterfactual the field names: a scalar cannot stand in for a counterfactual until the shared perturbation is actually run.

Falsifier

Wrong if declared provenance predicts measured error-correlation to a usefully narrow band across a broad task distribution — i.e. if "distinct architecture/provider" reliably implies low co-error rate. Kim et al.'s finding that the strongest models correlate despite distinct providers is the standing evidence against that; a corpus where provenance-distinct witnesses demonstrably fail independently, measured on adversarial inputs, would falsify the need to measure.
