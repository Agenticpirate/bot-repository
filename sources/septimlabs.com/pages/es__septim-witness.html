· the 18 red flags ·

What Witness actually checks for.

01

IP assignment scope

"Trabajo product" vs "all work of author." Retroactive assignments. Pre-existing IP carve-outs missing.

02

Indemnification direction

One-way (you indemnify them only) vs mutual. Third-party claims exposure. Cap amount.

03

Termination clause

Notice period both ways. Kill fees. Payment for work-in-progress on early termination.

04

Payment terms

Net-60 or Net-90. Late-fee interest rate. Deposit refundability. Milestone-vs-time basis.

05

Auto-renewal trap

Silent renewal with rolling price-hike rights. Notice window to opt out. Hidden multi-year extensions.

06

Non-compete scope

Duration, geographic, industry-wide. Non-solicit coverage of their clients vs all similar clients.

07

Confidentiality asymmetry

One-way NDA on them but mutual on you. Perpetual confidentiality vs time-limited. Residuals clause.

08

Venue / governing law

Their state vs neutral. Mandatory arbitration. Class-action waiver. Jury-trial waiver.

09

Warranties & disclaimers

Performance guarantees you can’t keep. Uptime SLAs on systems you don’t control. "Bug-free" promises.

10

Liquidated damages

Fixed-dollar penalties. Multiple-of-fee damages. Consequential damages exposure.

11

Scope creep protection

Change-order process. Written-approval requirement. Hourly overage rate. "Reasonable" ambiguity.

12

Exclusivity

Can you take other clients during the engagement? Right of first refusal on future work.

13

Subcontractor clause

Can you use subs? Client approval required? You’re liable for their work even if they’re independent.

14

Publicity & portfolio rights

Can you put their logo in your case studies? Blanket "confidential" clauses that silently block this.

15

Data ownership

Who owns analytics/telemetry/usage data? GDPR exposure? Data-return on termination.

16

Force majeure scope

Pandemic-era carve-outs still active? Client’s force-majeure rights vs yours.

17

Assignment rights

Can client sell the contract to a third party without your consent? Change-of-control triggers.

18

Surviving clauses

What clauses survive termination? Forever-confidentiality, non-compete after you’re gone.

· real example ·

A contract clause, before and after.

// original clause, from a real freelancer contract
"Contractor hereby assigns to Client all right, title, and interest
in and to all Trabajo Product, including any pre-existing materials,
derivatives thereof, and any improvements, modifications, or
enhancements made by Contractor during the Term."
Witness flags: "including any pre-existing materials" means any IP you had BEFOE signing this contract gets assigned to the client. If you reuse a library, a component, a utility function from your own portfolio — they own it now. That’s a career-ending clause.

Suggested rewrite: "Contractor assigns to Client all right, title, and interest in and to the Trabajo Product specifically created for Client under this Agreement, excluding any pre-existing materials of Contractor, which remain Contractor’s property and for which Contractor grants Client a perpetual, non-exclusive license solely for use within the Trabajo Product."

· critical disclaimer ·

Witness is not legal advice.

Septim Labs is not a law firm. Witness is a pattern-matching tool that runs your contract against common red-flag patterns. It does not replace a qualified attorney licensed in your jurisdiction. For contracts where the stakes are high enough that you’d normally get one reviewed by a lawyer, get it reviewed by a lawyer. Witness is designed for the 80% of freelancer contracts where the stakes are "I don’t want to read 9 pages tonight" — not the 20% where a real attorney is the right move.

Witness’s output is AI-generated and may contain false positives, false negatives, or misinterpretations. Always read your own contract. If a clause feels weird, don’t sign. If Witness flags something, verify it yourself before rejecting the clause to the client.
