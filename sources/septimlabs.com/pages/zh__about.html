// background

What the studio actually does.

I started Septim Labs after pulling up a client’s SaaS invoices on a shared screen. Fourteen line items. Three tools doing overlapping things. None of the data in accounts they controlled. The total was more than they’d paid a contractor two years earlier to build the thing those tools were supposed to replace — and that contractor had handed over the repository and walked away. The SaaS vendors had not.

The pitch has been the same since day one: pay once, own it. Hosting on a free tier (Vercel, Supabase, Cloudflare). Stripe for payments. Email through your own Gmail. No recurring vendor in the loop. When the build ships, your monthly cost is $0.

The studio is small — fewer than ten people. That is not a disclaimer; it’s the point. A small team finishes things. The person who specced your project ships it. The Claude Code toolchain is what makes the math work: it compresses weeks of boilerplate into days, which means fixed-fee custom builds actually pencil at prices that make sense for a shop owner or a solo founder — not just a venture-backed startup with a $200k engineering budget.

The name: Septim is from Latin septimus, seventh — September, the seventh month of the Roman calendar before Julius Caesar added two more. The studio opened in September. The Forge catalogue launched with seven pay-once tools. The number isn’t decorative. It was a commitment: finish seven things completely before adding an eighth.
