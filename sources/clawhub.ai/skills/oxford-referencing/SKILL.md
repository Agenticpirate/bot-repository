# Oxford Referencing

Build Oxford-style (documentary-note) citations: superscript footnote numbers in the text, full citations in footnotes, shortened notes thereafter, and an alphabetical bibliography. Also flags when a law paper actually needs OSCOLA instead.

## Steps

1. Read `references/oxford-quickref.md`, then place superscript note numbers at the end of the relevant clause or sentence, after punctuation. Every note number corresponds to a footnote.

2. First citation of a source, full form in the footnote:

   - Book: `Author, Title of Book (Place: Publisher, Year), p. X.`
   - Chapter: `Author, 'Chapter Title', in Editor (ed.), Book Title (Place: Publisher, Year), pp. xx-xx.`
   - Journal: `Author, 'Article Title', Journal Name, Volume, no. X (Year), pp. xx-xx.`
   - Web page: `Author, 'Page Title', Site Name, Day Month Year, URL.`

3. Subsequent citations, shortened form: `Author Surname, Short Title, p. X.` Use `ibid.` only when the note cites the same single source as the note immediately before it, and with a page number when the page differs.

4. Bibliography: alphabetical by author surname, full first-citation form without page numbers, `Place: Publisher, Year` retained. Every footnote source appears in the bibliography; the bibliography may also include background sources not footnoted, if the local guide allows.

5. Quotes: the footnote number follows the closing quotation mark; the page number lives in the footnote, not in the running text.

6. Law papers: Oxford general style is not OSCOLA. UK legal writing requires OSCOLA (citing cases as `Smith v Jones [2024] UKSC 1 [15]`, statutes as `Act Year`, section). Switch styles rather than forcing Oxford onto case law.

7. Verify: note numbers run in sequence with no gaps or reuse; every footnote resolves; first notes are full, later notes are shortened; bibliography matches.

## Pitfalls

- Oxford is footnote-based; do not mix in author-date parentheses. That is Harvard's mechanism.
- `ibid.` is fragile: inserting a note earlier in the document silently breaks every following `ibid.`. Prefer shortened notes in drafts that will be edited.
- Word processors auto-number footnotes; pasted text from other sources breaks the sequence. Renumber before submission.
- Web sources still need an access date in the footnote when content may change.
