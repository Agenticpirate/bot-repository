# Taste Ink Wash

A taste skill for the formal vocabulary of East Asian ink wash painting.

## Style Tokens

- Graduated ink tones from single brush loading
- Dry brush texture revealing paper grain
- Wet-on-wet diffusion for atmospheric effects
- Empty space as active compositional element
- Calligraphic line quality — pressure, speed, direction
- Bone method structural brushwork
- Ink-play spontaneous splashing
- Layered washes building tonal depth
- Mist and void suggesting infinite distance
- Single-stroke decisiveness without correction

## Anti-Tokens

- Heavy impasto or thick paint application
- Saturated chromatic color
- Hard mechanical edges
- Photographic detail or hyperrealism
- Symmetrical composition
- Fill every space with detail

## Application Rules

1. Treat empty space as the most important compositional decision
2. Build depth through ink tone variation, not linear perspective
3. Each brushstroke should be irreversible and decisive
4. Rhythm comes from alternating wet/dry, thick/thin, fast/slow
5. The paper itself is a participant, not merely a surface

## Evaluation Criteria

- Does the work breathe? Is there sufficient empty space?
- Can you sense the speed and pressure of each mark?
- Does tonal gradation create spatial depth without color?
- Is there a balance between control and spontaneity?
- Does the composition feel complete despite apparent emptiness?
