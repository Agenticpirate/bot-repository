# Chicago Referencing

Build Chicago style in either of its two systems, and pick the right one for the discipline:

- **Notes-bibliography** (NB): humanities, history, arts. Footnote numbers in text, full notes, bibliography.
- **Author-date** (AD): sciences and social sciences. Parenthetical `(Author Year, page)` citations, reference list.

## Steps

1. Read `references/chicago-quickref.md` and determine which system the assignment requires. Never mix systems in one document.

2. For **notes-bibliography**, place superscript note numbers after punctuation. First note, full form:
   - Book: `Author First Last, Title of Book (Place: Publisher, Year), 25.`
   - Chapter: `Author First Last, "Chapter Title," in Book Title, ed. Editor (Place: Publisher, Year), 45-52.`
   - Journal: `Author First Last, "Article Title," Journal Name Volume, no. X (Year): 33-35.`
   - Subsequent notes, shortened: `Last, Short Title, 26.` (`Ibid.` is deprecated in the 17th edition; prefer shortened citations.)

3. For **author-date**, cite parenthetically: `(Smith 2024, 12)` or narrative `Smith (2024, 12)`. Two authors: `(Smith and Jones 2024)`. Three or more: `(Smith et al. 2024)`. Reference list entries follow the bibliography form with the year moved up: `Smith, John. 2024. Title. Place: Publisher.`

4. Bibliography/References formatting (NB system):
   - Book: `Last, First. Title of Book. Place: Publisher, Year.`
   - Journal: `Last, First. "Article Title." Journal Name Volume, no. X (Year): xx-xx.`
   - Headline capitalization for titles; journal and book titles italic; article titles in quotation marks in bibliography
   - Alphabetical by author surname, hanging indent

5. Note punctuation vs bibliography punctuation differ in NB: notes use commas in the source description; bibliography uses periods. The quickref shows both forms side by side.

6. Verify: note numbers run in sequence; every note has a shortened-form ancestor; bibliography and notes cover the same sources; author-date texts have no footnotes.

## Pitfalls

- The two systems are not interchangeable; NB is humanities, AD is sciences. Check which one the instructor means by "Chicago".
- Page numbers live in the note `(Author First Last, Title, 25)` without `p.`; in author-date they sit after a comma: `(Smith 2024, 12)`.
- URLs in bibliography, not in notes, when the note already names the source clearly.
- Chicago allows `et al.` from four authors in notes and from one to ten authors listed in bibliography with `et al.` for eleven or more; check the edition in use.
