# Discussion Grammar Diagnosis

## Theory basis
*Science Research Writing* Unit 4.2.2 Q&A "Why is Sentence 2 in the Present Simple tense?" + "Why are Sentences 3 and 4 in the Past Simple tense?" + 4.5 (modal verb forms and tense).

## Tense principles (the single biggest grammar issue in Discussion)

| Tense | Use in Discussion | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | Permanent truths, generalisations, established facts | "Stress **disrupts** the intestinal barrier." |
| **Past Simple** | Study-specific findings (this paper) | "We **found** a significant difference..." |
| **Present Perfect** | Recently completed actions with present relevance (rare in Discussion) | "Several studies **have investigated** this question." |
| **Modal + bare infinitive** | Possibility / ability / recommendation (see Vocabulary skill) | "Stress **may disrupt**..." |

**Tense reflects the writer's confidence:**
- Present Simple = "I believe this is a permanent, independent truth"
- Past Simple = "this is what we observed in our study"

**Self-check:** Can you swap the verb between present and past without changing the claim? If yes, the tense choice encodes confidence, not time.

## What this skill diagnoses

1. **Tense–claim alignment** — Is the tense right for the writer's intended epistemic stance?
2. **Tense consistency** — Same move (e.g., revisit results) should use consistent tense unless there's a deliberate epistemic shift.
3. **Subject-verb agreement** — especially with long subjects (the results of the present study, the participants who completed the second task).
4. **Article usage** — a / an / the / zero article, especially with abstract nouns (the result, **a** significant result, results).
5. **Voice** — active vs passive; consistent choice within a sentence.
6. **Punctuation** — comma splices, run-ons, missing commas in compound/complex sentences, consistent decimal/thousand separators.

## Diagnostic signals

| Signal | Means |
|---|---|
| "Stress **disrupted** the intestinal barrier." (general claim, past tense) | Tense weakens claim — should be Present Simple |
| "The results of the present study **suggests**..." | Subject-verb agreement (study is singular; results is plural) |
| "It has been found that..." (no agent) | Unattributed passive — usually acceptable, but watch overuse |
| "**a** important finding" | Article error — should be "an" |
| "However the sample size was small" (no comma) | Punctuation — needs comma after "However" sentence-initial |
| Two independent clauses joined with comma only | Comma splice — needs semicolon, period, or conjunction |
