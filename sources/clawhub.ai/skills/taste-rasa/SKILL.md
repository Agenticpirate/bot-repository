# Taste Rasa
A taste skill derived from Abhinavagupta's rasa theory (Abhinavabharati, 11th century): art achieves aesthetic effect not through depicting individual emotion but through codified formal elements that trigger generalized aesthetic states (sadharanikarana) in the prepared viewer. Personal associations are burned away by the aesthetic frame, leaving only the pure flavor of an emotional register.
## Style Tokens
- Codified gesture as trigger — formalized movement carrying emotional weight
- Opposing registers simultaneous — multiple emotional valences in one composition
- Natural element as amplifier — landscape/weather serving state, not realism
- Ego-dissolution through form — personal identity suspended by aesthetic frame
- Generalized state over individual expression
- Balance of opposing forces — no single emotion dominates
- Prepared viewer assumption — the work rewards trained attention
- Aesthetic frame as boundary — clear separation from ordinary experience
- Flavor not narrative — tasting an emotional register, not following a story
- Nine-register spectrum — the full range from erotic to peaceful
## Anti-Tokens
- Individual psychology as subject matter
- Realistic emotional expression tied to specific persons
- Single dominant mood without counterbalance
- Narrative identification with characters
- Catharsis model — purging rather than savoring
- Subjective projection onto the work
- Uncodified spontaneity without formal structure
- Documentary emotion — recording rather than distilling
- Therapeutic art — using art to process personal feeling
- Personal confession as aesthetic content
## Application Rules
1. Identify the dominant rasa register: what generalized aesthetic state does the composition trigger (not what emotion the subject feels, but what the viewer tastes)?
2. Check for sadharanikarana: are personal associations dissolved by formal means (stylization, codification, abstraction)?
3. Check for opposing registers: does the composition hold multiple emotional valences simultaneously without one dominating?
4. Identify amplifiers: what formal elements serve as rasa-triggers rather than representational content?
5. The test: if you can name a specific person's specific emotion, sadharanikarana has not occurred
## Evaluation Criteria
- Does the work trigger a generalized aesthetic state rather than depict a specific emotion?
- Are formal elements codified enough to function as reliable triggers?
- Is there simultaneous presence of opposing registers creating richness?
- Does the aesthetic frame successfully dissolve personal identification?
- Can you taste the rasa without needing narrative context?
