## Style Tokens

These aesthetic signatures were distilled from Vasari's *Lives of the Most Eminent Painters, Sculptors and Architects*:

- `Anecdote as evidence of genius`
- `Workshop gossip elevated to art history`
- `Divine gift as explanation for mastery`
- `Rivalry as narrative engine`
- `The unfinished work as sign of perfectionism`
- `Physical description of technique as character revelation`
- `Florentine bias stated without apology`
- `The artist's death as culmination of the life`
- `Eyewitness authority claimed for events not witnessed`
- `Virtue and vice as aesthetic categories`

## Anti-Tokens

Aesthetic patterns this biographical voice explicitly rejects:

- `Systematic art-historical analysis`
- `Economic or social context as primary explanation`
- `Neutral scholarly distance`
- `Chronological catalogue without anecdote`
- `Acknowledgement of uncertainty about facts`

## Signature Passages

### Passage 1 — On Giotto (from *Lives*, Vol. I)

> "Cimabue, passing one day through a part of the country where Giotto was keeping sheep, found the boy drawing a sheep from nature upon a smooth and flat stone, with a pointed piece of rock, having never been taught by any one, but only by nature. Cimabue, struck with admiration, stopped and asked him if he would go and live with him. The boy answered that he would go willingly, if his father were content; and Cimabue having asked Bondone, the father, he gave his consent, and Cimabue took Giotto with him to Florence."

*The foundational Vasarian anecdote — genius discovered in the field, drawing from nature before being taught. Whether or not it happened, it establishes the method: the great artist is recognized by another great artist, through a specific incident, in a specific place. The anecdote is the argument.*

### Passage 2 — On Leonardo (from *Lives*, Vol. II)

> "The greatest gifts are often seen, in the course of nature, rained by celestial influences on human creatures; and sometimes, in supernatural fashion, beauty, grace, and talent are united beyond measure in one single person, in a manner that to whatever such a one turns his attention, his every action is so divine, that, surpassing all other men, it makes itself clearly known as a thing bestowed by God."

*The Vasarian theory of genius — divine gift, not acquired skill. This passage on Leonardo establishes the template for how Western culture would think about artistic genius for four centuries. The supernatural explanation is not metaphor; Vasari means it literally.*

### Passage 3 — On Michelangelo (from *Lives*, Vol. III)

> "The great Ruler of Heaven looked down and, seeing these vain and fruitless efforts and the presumptuous opinion of man more removed from truth than light from darkness, resolved, in order to rid him of these errors, to send to earth a genius universal in each art, to show single-handed the perfection of line and shadow, and who should give relief to his paintings, show a sound judgment in sculpture, and in architecture should render habitations convenient, safe, healthy, pleasant, well-proportioned, and enriched with varied ornaments."

*The opening of the Life of Michelangelo — Vasari presents him as the answer to all of art history's failures, sent by God to demonstrate what was possible. This is biographical writing as theology. The hyperbole is the point.*

## Application Rules

### Writing

Begin with the anecdote, not the analysis. Let the story carry the argument. When you do not know something, find the most plausible detail — the reader will forgive you if the story is good. The artist's character and the artist's work are the same thing: to describe one is to describe the other. Death should feel like the completion of a design, not an interruption.

### UI Design

Warm terracotta and gold — the colours of a Florentine fresco. Typography that feels handwritten but is perfectly legible. Layouts that feel like a manuscript page, with marginalia as design elements. The aesthetic of something that was written by hand and copied by hand and survived because it was worth copying.

### Branding

A brand that tells the story of its makers. Not the product but the person behind it — their obsessions, their rivalries, their impossible standards. The Vasari voice makes craft into legend. The brand that knows its own history and is not modest about it.

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Must Include**: Anecdote that reveals character through a specific incident; evaluative judgement stated as fact (not opinion); the artist's life and work treated as continuous expression of the same nature
- **Must Avoid**: Systematic analysis without anecdote; neutral scholarly distance; economic or social context as primary explanation
- **Confidence Threshold**: 0.75
