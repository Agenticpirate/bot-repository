## Style Tokens

- `gesture as narrative (mudrā)` — hand positions that communicate complete stories
- `canonical proportion (tālamāna)` — mathematical body ratios prescribed by tradition
- `bronze lost-wax precision` — technical mastery as devotional offering
- `divine multiplicity` — additional arms as narrative compression, not decoration
- `dynamic stillness (tribhaṅga)` — triple-bend posture creating life within balance
- `flame aureole (prabhāmaṇḍala)` — cosmic context framing the figure
- `sacred geometry in base` — lotus or geometric pedestal as cosmological ground
- `surface texture as devotional offering` — polish and finish as spiritual labor
- `frontality with subtle asymmetry` — facing the viewer while remaining alive
- `iconographic attribute system` — objects held identify the deity precisely

## Anti-Tokens

- `naturalistic anatomy`
- `casual posture`
- `secular narrative`
- `rough surface texture`
- `asymmetric chaos`
- `minimalist abstraction`

## Application Rules

1. Every gesture (mudrā) must be narratively complete — a viewer unfamiliar with the tradition should sense that the hand position means something specific
2. Proportion follows canonical rules (tālamāna) — deviation from these proportions signals a different tradition, not creative freedom
3. Dynamic stillness: the figure appears to move while remaining perfectly balanced — tribhaṅga (triple-bend) creates life without instability
4. Multiplicity serves narrative compression — each additional arm carries a specific attribute, not decorative repetition
5. Surface finish is devotional labor — the smoothness of bronze or polish of stone is itself an offering

## Evaluation Criteria

- Does the gesture communicate narrative without text?
- Does the proportion feel canonical rather than naturalistic?
- Is there dynamic stillness — movement within perfect balance?
- Does multiplicity serve meaning rather than decoration?
- Does the surface quality suggest devotional care in making?
