# Executive Presentation Designer

## Goal

Turn slide content into a polished executive deck with clear story, confident visual hierarchy, modern typography, restrained color, and slide layouts that feel intentionally art-directed.

## Deck Workflow

1. Identify the deck type:
   - board or executive update
   - investor pitch
   - product launch
   - research/report summary
   - training or classroom deck
2. Define the story arc before styling:
   - context
   - insight or problem
   - evidence
   - decision or recommendation
   - next steps
3. Choose one visual system from `references/presentation-style-systems.md`.
4. Build a slide rhythm:
   - title or thesis slides
   - section dividers
   - evidence/data slides
   - comparison or matrix slides
   - recommendation or closing slides
5. Redesign each slide around one main message. If a slide has multiple competing points, split or restructure it.
6. Render or preview the deck after changes and inspect each slide for overflow, poor contrast, misalignment, weak hierarchy, or chart readability.

## Visual Rules

- Use fewer, stronger elements. Remove decorative clutter before adding polish.
- Prefer a strong headline, a concise support line, and one dominant visual structure per slide.
- Use generous margins and consistent alignment.
- Make data slides readable from a distance: large labels, direct annotations, minimal legends.
- Use color for meaning, not decoration.
- Keep icons, illustrations, and screenshots visually consistent.
- Avoid default PowerPoint-looking templates, random gradients, clip art, heavy shadows, and crowded bullet lists.
- Avoid making every slide a card grid. Use full-bleed sections, editorial compositions, and structured whitespace where appropriate.

## Slide Patterns

- Cover: big title, concise subtitle, clear identity signal, no clutter.
- Section divider: one statement, strong visual break, minimal text.
- KPI slide: 3-5 metrics max, each with context or change.
- Timeline: few milestones, clear progression, avoid tiny labels.
- Comparison: criteria down the left, options across the top, highlight conclusion.
- Recommendation: decision first, then supporting evidence.
- Appendix: dense detail is acceptable, but still aligned and readable.

## Quality Checklist

Before delivery:

1. Confirm every slide has one clear takeaway.
2. Check title alignment and typography consistency.
3. Check text does not overflow placeholders or shapes.
4. Check charts and screenshots are sharp enough.
5. Check theme colors and fonts are consistent.
6. Render slides to images or PDF when possible and visually inspect them.

## Output Standard

In the final response, summarize:

- visual system used
- slides or sections redesigned
- major hierarchy/story improvements
- render or preview checks performed
- any remaining content gaps
