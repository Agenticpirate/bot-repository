## Cross-Domain Exemplars

### Painting — Holbein, *The Ambassadors* (1533)

The two ambassadors stand surrounded by instruments of learning and power. Between them, at an angle that requires the viewer to move, is an anamorphic skull — invisible until you step to the side. Time is present in the painting but only visible from the right position. This is the Vanitas tradition at its most sophisticated: the skull is not a symbol but a structural fact about how the painting works.

### Poetry — Keats, *Ode on a Grecian Urn* (1819)

> "Thou still unravish'd bride of quietness, / Thou foster-child of silence and slow time..."

The urn is outside time — the figures on it will never complete their movement, never age, never die. Keats makes this permanence both beautiful and terrible. "Beauty is truth, truth beauty" — the urn's eternity is purchased at the cost of life. Time arrested is also time denied.

### Philosophy — Ecclesiastes (c. 450–200 BCE)

> "Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher, vanity of vanities; all is vanity. What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun?"

The oldest and most direct statement of time's verdict. Not nihilism — the Preacher goes on to say that eating and drinking and finding satisfaction in work is good. But the frame is time: everything passes, and the question of what remains is the question that all art must answer.

## Application Rules

### Writing

Let the past be present in the object. The skull in the still life is not a symbol — it is a fact about the flowers. Let time accumulate in the description: the painting that has darkened, the letter that has faded, the garden that has grown over the path. The most powerful images of time are specific, not general. Do not say "time passes" — show what has been left behind.

### UI Design

Warm aged tones — the colour of old paper, old wood, old gold. Typography that has weight and history. Layouts that feel considered rather than current. The aesthetic of something that was made to last. Patina is not a flaw but evidence of duration.

### Branding

A brand that acknowledges it exists in time. Not timeless — time-aware. The brand voice should feel like something that will still make sense in fifty years. The patina of age is not a flaw but evidence of duration. What has lasted, and why?

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Must Include**: Object or image that makes time visible (ruin, skull, stopped clock, faded material); the past present in the current moment, not as nostalgia but as fact; mortality or transience acknowledged without sentimentality
- **Must Avoid**: Time as linear progress toward improvement; nostalgia as the primary register; the present moment treated as self-sufficient
- **Confidence Threshold**: 0.75
